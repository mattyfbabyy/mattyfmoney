Questions I get every week

Tap one. Straight answers, no runaround.

Do I actually need life insurance?

If someone would be in a worse spot financially if your income stopped, yes. A partner, kids, a parent you help, a co-signer on a loan. If nobody depends on you, you may not need it yet, and I'll tell you that.

Term vs. whole life. What's the difference?

Term covers you for a set number of years and then ends. Cheap, simple, does one job. Whole life covers you for life and builds cash value you can use along the way. It costs more because it does more.

A lot of people end up with some of each. The mix depends on your age, your family, and what you want the money to do.

How much coverage do I need?

Start with your income, how many years it would need to be replaced, and what you'd want paid off. That gives a real number. "Ten times your salary" is a starting point, not an answer; two people with the same salary can need very different amounts.

We'll figure out your number on the call.

I have coverage through work. Isn't that enough?

Usually not. Group coverage is often one or two times salary, and it typically ends when the job does. It's a nice bonus, not a plan.

What is cash value?

The part of a permanent policy that grows over time and belongs to you. You can borrow against it or use it while you're alive. It's a long game and it's not the right tool for everyone. Anyone who tells you it always is or never is isn't giving you the full picture.

I rent. Does this apply to me?

Yes. Coverage isn't about owning a home. It's about who depends on your income. If someone does, this applies.

Am I too young to worry about this?

Coverage is priced on age and health. The younger and healthier you are when you set it up, the less it costs, and you lock that in. Waiting rarely makes it cheaper.

What does it cost to work with you?

The call is free. If you set up a policy, I'm paid by the insurance company, not by you writing me a check. I'll explain exactly how that works before you decide anything.

Will you pressure me?

No. If the plan is right, it'll still be right next week. I spent ten years in real estate watching people get pushed into decisions. I don't do that.
